The Australian Schizophrenia Research Bank (ASRB) is a case-control resource set up in 2006 by the Schizophrenia
Research Institute with university and health service partners in Newcastle, Brisbane, Perth and Melbourne. NeuRA dates
its launch to 2007. It linked clinical, neuropsychological, genetic and brain imaging information from people with schizophrenia and healthy
controls, and also served as a pool of volunteers for later studies. Researchers could apply for data from 2009. NeuRA,
which has hosted the bank since 2015, states that the ASRB was retired in late 2022 and that de-identified, unlinkable
raw data remain available to researchers with ethics approval.

## Composition

Cases have a confirmed DSM-IV or ICD-10 diagnosis of schizophrenia or schizoaffective disorder. Controls were screened
for a personal or family history of psychiatric illness. Participants were English-speaking adults aged 18 to 65. The
2010 descriptor paper reports preliminary analyses of 493 cases and 293 controls, with mean ages of 39.5 and 37.4 years.
Men made up 65.9% of cases and 46.8% of controls. NeuRA describes the full bank as covering more than 1,000 people.
Only a subset of participants consented to and completed MRI, and the number scanned is not stated in the sources.

## Acquisition

Recruitment ran in Sydney, the Hunter, Illawarra, Orange, Canberra, Brisbane, Perth and Melbourne. According to the
2013 researcher guidelines, consenting participants had two scans on a 1.5 T Siemens Avanto: a T1-weighted MP-RAGE with
0.98 x 0.98 x 1.0 mm voxels, and a diffusion tensor scan with 64 directions at b = 1000 s/mm2 plus one b = 0 image.
Every participant also had a diagnostic interview, a cognitive battery and a blood sample for DNA.

## Annotations

There are no image labels. Each subject comes with a diagnosis and a core set of age, sex and ethnicity; clinical
interview and cognitive scores are released on justified request. Processed images may be available from the bank.

## Known limitations

- The bank is retired. The most recent published access terms are the 2013 guidelines, and current conditions must be
  confirmed with NeuRA.
- Access went through an access committee and carried cost recovery fees (in 2013, $800 per application plus $1,000
  and $15 per participant for MRI/DTI data).
- The cases differ from population samples, for example by sex ratio and education, as the descriptor paper notes.
