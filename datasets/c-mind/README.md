C-MIND (Cincinnati MR Imaging of NeuroDevelopment) is a pediatric MRI study started in 2009 by the Pediatric
Functional Neuroimaging Research Network, which the Pediatric Neuroimaging Research Consortium at Cincinnati
Children's Hospital Medical Center led under an NICHD contract. Its goals were to standardize how children from birth
through adolescence are recruited, scanned and processed, and to describe normal brain development across that age
range, with a focus on language and attention. The study's website now states that NIH funding has ended and that all
data moved to NIMH Data Archive (NDA) collection 2329.

## Composition

NDA lists 211 shared subjects, all of whom have records in the Image data structure. The study describes the cohort
as more than 200 typically developing children aged 0 to 18 years in a cross-sectional design, plus longitudinal data
from 40 infants and toddlers (0 to 3 years) and 30 children aged 7 to 9, scanned in consecutive years. NDA does not
break the shared subjects down by age, sex or visit. Behavioral structures cover intelligence scales (WPPSI-IV,
WISC, WAIS-IV, Bayley-III for infants), vocabulary, reading, memory, attention, motor and behavior checklists, each
shared for 7 to 210 subjects.

## Acquisition

According to the NDA and NITRC descriptions, the imaging includes 3D T1- and T2-weighted anatomy, DTI and HARDI
diffusion, resting-state fMRI, baseline ASL perfusion and a combined ASL/BOLD acquisition during two language tasks
(story listening and sentence-picture matching). A study paper reports a Philips 3T Achieva scanner with a 32-channel
head coil at Cincinnati Children's. Infants were scanned during natural sleep without sedation; older children were
awake and could watch a movie. The funder's interview mentions children from the Cincinnati and Los Angeles areas.

## Known limitations

- Access requires an approved NDA Data Access Request and Data Use Certification.
- The NDA page gives no counts per sequence, age group, sex or session, so only the total is listed.
- The scanner model comes from one study paper on a subset of 113 children and may not describe every session or
  site.
- The last submission to the NDA collection was in March 2016, and its expected-data list is marked not completed.
