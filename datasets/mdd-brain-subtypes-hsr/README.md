A single-centre cohort of 102 adults with major depressive disorder from IRCCS Ospedale San Raffaele in Milan. The
study asked whether patients can be split into biologically meaningful subgroups from brain structure alone. Regional
cortical thickness, grey matter volumes and white matter fractional anisotropy were harmonized across scanners,
reduced in dimension and clustered without labels. The two resulting groups were then compared on treatment
resistance, symptom profile, childhood trauma and blood immune-inflammatory markers. The authors deposited the
feature tables, clinical variables and cluster labels on Mendeley Data under CC BY-NC 3.0. The MR images are not
available.

## Composition

102 patients, 69 women and 33 men, aged 23 to 65 years (mean 49.4), counted from the open clinical table. 31 are
marked as treatment-resistant. Clustering gave one group of 59 patients with more treatment resistance, more
energy-related symptoms, more reported childhood abuse and emotional neglect, and thinner cortex; and one group of 43
patients with cognitive and affective symptoms and thicker cortex. The clinical table also holds education, illness
onset, number of episodes, medication load, BMI, HDRS-21, BDI subscales and CTQ subscales.

## Acquisition

Structural and diffusion MRI were acquired at San Raffaele. A scanner column in the clinical table separates two
scanners (46 patients on the older, 56 on the newer one), which is why the features were harmonized. Field strength,
vendor and sequence parameters are in the paper's methods, which are not openly readable, so they are left out here.

## Annotations

There are no image annotations. The labels are the data-driven cluster assignment per patient and the clinical
variables listed above.

## Known limitations

- Images are not shared; only about 400 regional features per patient are open.
- Patients only, no healthy controls in the open files.
- Single centre with a scanner change during recruitment.
- Inflammatory marker values are not part of the open files.
