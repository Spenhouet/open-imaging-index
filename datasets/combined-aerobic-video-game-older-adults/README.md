This Science Data Bank record holds the imaging and test data of a trial at the Institute of Psychology, Chinese Academy of Sciences, in Beijing (ChiCTR1900022702). The trial asked whether pairing aerobic exercise with a cognitively rich video game improves mnemonic discrimination in older adults more than either training alone, and which brain changes go with it. Healthy, right-handed, community-dwelling older adults were scanned before and after 16 weeks. The record was first published on 10 February 2026 and updated to version 2 on 9 March 2026. It is one of three trial datasets described together in a Scientific Data paper; the other two cover square dance and multimodal interventions.

## Composition

98 participants were enrolled and allocated without randomisation to combined training (21), aerobic cycling (22), video game training (22) or a control group that kept its usual lifestyle (21), plus 12 controls who completed only the first scan. Inclusion asked for age 53 to 80, at least five years of schooling, a Mini-Mental State Examination score of 24 or more and no MRI contraindications. After quality control, 86 had two usable resting-state scans: 65 trained and 21 controls, 70 of them women, with mean ages of 63.1 (trained) and 63.3 years (controls). The release is a BIDS folder with defaced T1-weighted images and resting-state fMRI per session, MRIQC reports and conversion code, plus a spreadsheet with group, time point, age, sex, education and test scores (MoCA, digit span, paired associate learning, verbal fluency, anxiety and depression scales).

## Acquisition

All scans come from one 3 T GE Discovery MR750 with an 8-channel head coil. The T1-weighted scan is a 3D sequence with 1 mm isotropic voxels. Resting-state fMRI uses echo-planar imaging with TR 2000 ms, 3.5 mm isotropic voxels and 240 volumes.

## Known limitations

- Group allocation followed participant preference and schedules, so self-selection may bias group comparisons.
- Sex and age are reported only for the 86 participants with two usable scans, not for all 98.
- Use is limited to research and education by the terms file in the record, although it also names CC BY 4.0.
- One site and one population of Chinese older adults.
