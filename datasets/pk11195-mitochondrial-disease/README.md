A case-control PET-MRI study from the University of Cambridge that imaged the 18 kDa translocator protein (TSPO), an
outer mitochondrial membrane protein, with the radioligand [11C]PK11195 in people with genetically confirmed
mitochondrial disease. The aim was to see whether TSPO binding can serve as a noninvasive brain biomarker of
mitochondrial pathology, rather than only as a marker of neuroinflammation. The images are not public: the paper states
that raw data are available from the corresponding author on request. A Dryad record (mirrored on Zenodo) holds only
supplementary figures and per-patient statistical map overlays.

## Composition

Fifteen patients from a specialist mitochondrial disease clinic at Addenbrooke's Hospital consented; one scan was
cancelled, leaving 14. Thirteen were symptomatic and one was an asymptomatic carrier from the family of another
patient. Mutations covered both the mitochondrial genome (for example MT-TL1 m.3243A>G, MT-ND6, MT-ND4, MT-ATP6) and
nuclear genes (POLG, SPG7, TYMP), with syndromes such as MELAS, MIDD, MNGIE, Leber hereditary optic neuropathy and
ataxia. The 33 healthy controls, matched for age and sex, came from a depression study (25) and a Parkinson disease
study (8) that used the same protocol on the same scanner.

## Acquisition

All participants had one session on a GE Signa PET-MR at 3 T. [11C]PK11195 was injected as a bolus and list-mode PET
was recorded for 75 minutes, then reconstructed with time-of-flight OSEM at 2.0 x 2.0 x 2.8 mm voxels. A 1 mm
isotropic T1-weighted volume acquired during PET was used for registration and region definition, and T1- and
T2-weighted images were read clinically. Binding potential was computed with a simplified reference tissue model after
partial volume correction.

## Annotations

No image annotations are described. Patients carry clinical labels: mutation, syndrome, modified Rankin Scale and
Scale for the Assessment and Rating of Ataxia scores, and radiology findings.

## Known limitations

The patient group is small and genetically heterogeneous. Controls were drawn from two other studies; the depression
controls share their ethics approval with the BIODEP study. Access depends on the authors, and no terms of use are
published.
