This entry covers a retrospective brain MRI cohort from the medical oncology intensive care unit of Samsung Medical
Center in Seoul. Between March 2010 and February 2014, adult cancer patients who developed acute neurological deficits
in the unit had brain MRI because a cerebral infarction was suspected. Two papers by the same group analyse the cohort:
one describes the clinical picture and diffusion lesion patterns of stroke in these patients, the other asks whether
D-dimer levels help to diagnose infarction or point to a cryptogenic cause. The MRI images are not public. Harvard
Dataverse hosts, under CC0 1.0, one spreadsheet with a row per patient.

## Composition

Of 2258 patients admitted to the unit in the study period, 88 met the inclusion criteria: 33 with solid tumors and 55
with hematologic malignancies, 51 of them men. Infarction was diagnosed in 43 patients, most often as multiple lesions
across arterial territories, and 7 had central nervous system metastases. The age rows in the statistics are counted
from the released spreadsheet.

## Acquisition

The BMC Cancer paper names two scanners used for brain MRI at the center: a 1.5 T GE Signa with a quadrature head coil
(1997 to 2010) and a 3 T Philips Achieva with an eight-channel head coil (2006 to 2014). It does not say which patient
was scanned on which system. Infarction was defined by high signal on diffusion-weighted imaging with reduced apparent
diffusion coefficient. Vessel imaging (non-contrast or contrast-enhanced MR angiography, or CT angiography) and
echocardiography were used to classify stroke etiology, but the papers do not give how many patients had each.

## Annotations

Two neurologists and a neuroradiologist read every scan. The spreadsheet records the stroke diagnosis, lesion location,
DWI lesion pattern, TOAST stroke mechanism and the main finding in patients without infarction. No image annotations are
released.

## Known limitations

- MRI images are not shared, and no terms for them are published.
- Single center, retrospective, and limited to patients in whom stroke was suspected.
- The median age computed from the spreadsheet (64) differs from the 63 stated in both papers.
