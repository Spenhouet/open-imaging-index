This OpenNeuro dataset comes from the Mackey Lab at the University of Pennsylvania. It was shared with a 2022 study
that asked whether early stress, measured as family socioeconomic status, stressful life events and observed
parenting, relates to how children's brains respond to emotional and social moments in a film. Children watched the
five-minute Pixar short Piper, which has no dialogue and shows a young sandpiper and its parent, while functional MRI
was recorded. Snapshot 1.0.1 (October 2022) is released under a CC0 waiver.

## Composition

There are 70 typically developing children aged 4 to 11 (mean 8.4 years), 48 boys and 22 girls by parent report.
They were recruited in and around Philadelphia through two larger studies: 30 from a study of environmental
influences on brain development, and 40 typically developing controls from a study of autism at the Children's
Hospital of Philadelphia. Of 82 children scanned, 12 were excluded for incomplete movie scans, a registration error
or a newly reported ADHD diagnosis. A phenotype table gives age, gender, race, ethnicity, parental education, income,
stressful life events, parenting ratings, motion summaries and amygdala response values.

## Acquisition

All scans were done on a 3 T Siemens Prisma with a 32-channel head coil. Each child has one multi-echo MPRAGE
structural scan with prospective motion correction and one multiband gradient-echo EPI run (2 mm isotropic, TR 2 s,
150 volumes) recorded during the movie.

## Annotations

There are no image annotations. An events file marks the movie segments with positive affect, negative affect and
parent-child interaction.

## Known limitations

- Children from the autism-study arm (40) watched the movie without sound; the others heard the soundtrack.
- Parenting behaviour was observed only in the 30 children from the first study.
- One session per child, with no follow-up and no adult comparison group.
- Only the final analysis sample is released; excluded children are not included.
