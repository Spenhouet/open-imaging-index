QIN-PROSTATE is a small collection of multiparametric prostate MRI exams from Brigham and Women's Hospital, contributed
by the group of Fiona Fennessy within the NCI Quantitative Imaging Network (grant U01 CA151261). The Cancer Imaging
Archive published it in 2014 to support the development and testing of quantitative methods for characterizing prostate
cancer. Its authors used part of it to compare pharmacokinetic models of dynamic contrast-enhanced MRI. TCIA retired the collection on 13 April
2026, so the images can no longer be downloaded from the archive.

## Composition

The collection covers 22 patients with prostate cancer. The MRI protocol includes T1-weighted, T2-weighted, diffusion-weighted
and DCE series. Study, series and image counts, demographics and the download size are not shown on the collection page
since its retirement. A subset of 17 patients with histologically confirmed cancer was analysed by Fedorov et al. (2014);
that subset had a mean age of 58 years (range 42 to 74) and Gleason scores from 3+3 to 4+5. The age and grade of the whole collection are not reported.

## Acquisition

All exams were acquired at 3 T on a GE Signa HDx scanner with an 8-channel abdominal array combined with an endorectal
coil. T1-weighted images used a spoiled gradient echo sequence and T2-weighted images a fast recovery fast spin echo
sequence, both over a 16 cm field of view. Diffusion was measured with echo planar imaging at b-values of 0 and 500
s/mm², suitable for an ADC map. The DCE series used a 3D spoiled gradient echo sequence covering the whole gland with
reconstructed voxels of 1×1×6 mm and a frame interval of roughly 5 seconds. About five frames were recorded before
gadopentetate dimeglumine (0.15 mmol/kg) was injected at 3 ml/s.

## Annotations

The collection page lists no segmentations or other annotations. In the 2014 study, an abdominal radiologist outlined
tumor regions of interest in 3D Slicer, but these are not described as part of the shared collection.

## Known limitations

- The collection is retired and no longer distributed by TCIA, and its license is no longer shown on the collection
  page.
- Single site, single scanner and a small cohort.
- No clinical or pathology data are described for the full collection.
