This cohort comes from an NIMH-funded randomized trial at the Yale Child Study Center (NCT01965184, grant R01MH101514) that compared 12 sessions of cognitive-behavioral therapy with 12 sessions of supportive psychotherapy for irritability, anger and aggression in children and adolescents. The study follows the NIMH Research Domain Criteria approach: aggression is treated as a dimension that cuts across diagnoses, and the trial asks whether change in emotion regulation circuitry tracks response to therapy. Children were scanned with fMRI and recorded with EEG before treatment, and the trial protocol planned a second session at the end of treatment. The data are held in NIMH Data Archive collection 2139, which lists 96 shared subjects.

## Composition

The baseline analysis by Ibrahim et al. includes 100 children with aggressive behavior (27 girls) and 29 healthy controls (13 girls), aged 8 to 16, with a mean age of 11.9 years. Of 136 children scanned, 7 were excluded for head motion or a computer error. All children in the aggression group scored at least 65 on the CBCL Aggressive Behavior scale and met criteria for oppositional defiant disorder, conduct disorder or disruptive mood dysregulation disorder. Co-occurring diagnoses were common: 78 had ADHD, 26 an anxiety disorder and 18 autism spectrum disorder. Controls had no psychiatric or neurological history. The trial registry reports 101 enrolled participants.

## Acquisition

Imaging used a Siemens MAGNETOM Tim Trio at 3 T. The analysed run is a 4.7 minute block-design task of fearful and calm faces from the NimStim set, during which children judged the gender of each face. The NDA collection description also names a frustration-inducing GoNoGo task in fMRI and EEG; its scan counts are not reported in the paper.

## Annotations

There are no image labels. Subjects carry K-SADS-PL diagnoses, CBCL, Reactive-Proactive Aggression Questionnaire, SRS-2 and WASI IQ scores, and the trial adds treatment arm and outcome ratings.

## Known limitations

Structural scan details, the follow-up scans and the EEG are described in a supplement or the protocol and are not broken down here. The NDA collection shares fewer subjects than the paper analysed, and which runs it contains is not listed publicly. The control group is small and the sample comes from one site.
