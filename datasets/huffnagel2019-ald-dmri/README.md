This entry describes the imaging cohort of a prospective study at Amsterdam UMC that followed men with
adrenoleukodystrophy (ALD) and healthy controls with diffusion MRI. The aim was to find out whether diffusion metrics of
the cervical spinal cord and of the corticospinal tracts in the brain change measurably as myelopathy progresses, so
that future trials could run with fewer patients or shorter follow-up. The MRI images are not part of any public
release. Dryad hosts, under CC0, the paper's supplementary tables with enrollment numbers, group-level change scores and
per-patient diffusion and clinical values.

## Composition

The paper reports usable data for 52 patients and 33 controls. According to the enrollment table, 61 patients and 35
controls were screened. Patients had up to three brain MR sessions (52, 43 and 36 at sessions one to three) and up to two
spinal cord sessions (41 and 23). Controls had one session each: 33 brain and 30 spinal cord scans. The longitudinal
analysis covers 36 patients over two years. Exclusion reasons listed in the table include age limits, cerebral ALD, MRI
contraindications, braces, technical problems and loss to follow-up.

## Acquisition

Diffusion MRI of the brain and of the cervical spinal cord. Fractional anisotropy, mean, axial and radial diffusivity
were derived. Spinal cord imaging was added to the protocol after the study had started, so early patients have fewer
spinal cord sessions, and a spinal cord session falls on either the first or second brain session. Scanner, field
strength and sequence parameters are given in the paper's full text, which is behind a subscription and was not read.

## Annotations

No image annotations are released. Clinical scores for myelopathy were collected alongside imaging: Expanded Disability
Status Scale, Severity Scoring System for Progressive Myelopathy, Timed Up-and-Go and the 6-Minute Walk Test.

## Known limitations

- Images and diffusion maps are not shared, and no terms for them are published.
- Controls were scanned once, so there is no longitudinal control data.
- Only men with ALD were studied; the sex of controls is not stated in the abstract.
- Acquisition details could not be verified from an open source and are left out.
