This entry describes the PET cohort behind a Neurology paper from the University of Rennes and Rennes University Hospital in France. The authors used resting-state FDG-PET of patients with Parkinson's disease to test whether cerebellar glucose metabolism tracks how impaired patients are in motor, cognitive and psychiatric domains, and which supratentorial regions co-vary with the cerebellar clusters they found. The images themselves are not public. The deposit on Dryad, mirrored on Zenodo under CC0, holds only the supplementary document of the paper.

## Composition

The analysis included 90 patients with Parkinson's disease. The supplement compares them with 27 patients who were excluded, and reports a mean disease duration of 10.7 years (SD 4.4) in the included group. Each patient had clinical motor scales (UPDRS parts, Hoehn and Yahr, Schwab and England), a neuropsychological battery (Stroop, Trail Making, verbal fluency, Modified Card Sorting) and psychiatric ratings. These scores were reduced with principal component analysis per domain.

## Acquisition

Each patient had one resting-state brain PET with [18F]FDG. The scanner, reconstruction and patient medication state during the scan are described in the paper, which could not be opened for this entry, so they are not listed here.

## Annotations

There are no image annotations. The per-subject data are clinical scores and principal component values used as regressors against voxelwise cerebellar metabolism.

## Known limitations

The images and the subject-level clinical table are not shared, and no request process or terms are published in the deposit. Counts here come from the abstract and the supplement; sex and age breakdowns are left out because they could not be read from an openly accessible source.
