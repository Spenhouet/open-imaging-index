This release holds the movie-watching functional MRI from a study at the National Institute of Mental Health (NIMH)
on how young people with autism and typically developing peers orient to and make sense of social scenes. Ramot et al.
(2020) measured how closely each person's brain response to the movie matched the group average, and compared that with
how typical their eye movements were on a separate set of short clips watched outside the scanner. The fMRI data were
posted on NIH figshare in January 2020 under CC BY 4.0.

## Composition

99 participants aged 15 to 30: 36 males with autism and 63 typically developing participants, 24 of them female. Mean
age was 20.7 in the autism group and 22.05 in the typically developing group. Autism diagnoses rested on DSM-IV
criteria with ADI-R and/or ADOS cutoffs given by a research-reliable clinician, and all full-scale IQ scores were above
94. The paper analysed 62 of the typically developing participants after excluding one for abnormal brain structure,
and picked 36 of them as a male control group matched on age, IQ and head motion.

## Acquisition

Scans were acquired on a GE MR-750 3 T system with a 32-channel head coil. Each participant has a structural MPRAGE
and one multi-echo EPI run (TR 2 s, 3 mm voxels, three echoes at 17.5, 35.3 and 53.1 ms, 28 slices, 285 volumes)
recorded during a 9.5 minute scene from The Princess Bride with sound. The slice coverage did not include the whole
brain, with parts of motor cortex missing. Files are anonymised NIfTI, one per echo, packed in three archives.

## Annotations

No image annotations. Group membership (autism or typically developing) is encoded in the file names. Eye tracking
recordings from the same participants are separate records in the figshare collection.

## Known limitations

- The autism group is all male, so sex is confounded with diagnosis unless the matched male control subset is used.
- Partial field of view in the functional runs.
- The figshare record does not include a participant table; age, sex and IQ per subject are not part of this release
  as far as the record describes.
- One typically developing participant has abnormal brain structure and was left out of the published analyses.
