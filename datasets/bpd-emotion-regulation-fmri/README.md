This entry covers the cohort of a multicentre task fMRI study run by Maastricht University with partners in Freiburg,
Lübeck and Hamburg, published in 2018 in the Journal of Psychiatry & Neuroscience. Women with borderline personality
disorder (BPD), women with cluster-C personality disorder and nonpatient controls looked at emotional pictures and,
in other trials, tried to regulate their reaction by reminding themselves that they were safe. The study asked whether
BPD shows heightened emotional sensitivity and weaker regulation, and whether this is specific to BPD. The scans are
not public. A CC0 deposit on DataverseNL holds the group-level BrainVoyager results.

## Composition

141 women were scanned: 62 with BPD, 48 controls and 31 with cluster-C personality disorder. After exclusions for
scanning or clinical criteria, 55, 42 and 24 entered the analysis. Mean age was 28 to 31 years per group, within an
inclusion range of 18 to 65. Most BPD patients also had major depressive disorder (49 of 55) and about two thirds took
antidepressants; 17 of the 24 cluster-C patients had avoidant personality disorder.

## Acquisition

Participants were recruited at two Dutch sites (Maastricht, Heerlen) and three German sites (Freiburg, Lübeck,
Hamburg) and scanned on 3 T systems. The paper does not name the scanner vendors; detailed parameters sit in an
appendix and in a restricted file of the deposit. The task had 96 trials in four runs, using negative, neutral,
positive and erotic social pictures. Analysis used BrainVoyager QX 2.6.

## Annotations

There are no image labels. Diagnoses come from structured clinical interviews, and the paper reports symptom
questionnaires, estimated IQ, handedness and medication per group.

## Known limitations

- Individual functional and anatomical MRI are not shared. The open archive holds only group-level GLMs, contrast
  maps, an averaged anatomy, a mask and region-of-interest time courses in BrainVoyager formats.
- Women only, so results do not generalise to men.
- Patients had varied comorbidities and medication.
- Per-site counts and scanner models are not reported.
