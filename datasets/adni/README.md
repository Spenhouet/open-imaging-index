ADNI is a long-running observational study, set up like a clinical trial, that tracks how imaging, fluid biomarkers
and cognition change from normal ageing through mild cognitive impairment (MCI) to Alzheimer's dementia. Started in
2004 as a public-private partnership led by Michael Weiner, it is among the most used neuroimaging resources in
dementia research. This entry covers the phases ADNI1, ADNI GO, ADNI2 and ADNI3; ADNI4 is ongoing.

## Composition

A 2023 ADNI review counts 868 cognitively unimpaired participants, 1,090 with MCI and 408 with Alzheimer's dementia
enrolled since 2004. Each phase recruited new people and carried forward many earlier participants: ADNI1 began with
819 people (229 controls, 398 MCI, 192 AD, mean age about 75), ADNI GO added an early MCI group and ADNI2 added a
subjective memory concern group. People are seen repeatedly, so most have several imaging sessions over years.
Clinical, cognitive, genetic and CSF/blood biomarker data come with the images.

## Acquisition

More than 60 sites in the US and Canada take part, using GE, Philips and Siemens scanners with harmonised protocols.
ADNI1 scanned everyone at 1.5 T (T1-weighted and dual-echo PD/T2), with a quarter also at 3 T. From ADNI GO on,
imaging moved to 3 T and added FLAIR and T2*-weighted scans; diffusion, resting-state fMRI and ASL were added per
vendor in ADNI2 and for nearly all participants in ADNI3. PET started with FDG and a small PiB amyloid pilot, then
used florbetapir for amyloid and, from ADNI3, flortaucipir (AV-1451) for tau. PET images are harmonised to a common
resolution and both raw and processed versions are released.

## Annotations

There are no manual image labels. ADNI provides diagnoses at each visit, cognitive scores, quality control ratings and
derived numbers such as FreeSurfer volumes and regional amyloid and tau SUVRs from the imaging cores.

## Known limitations

- Diagnostic criteria and group names changed between phases (early/late MCI were merged again in ADNI3).
- Scanner upgrades over the years can break longitudinal comparability; the MRI core advises treating data before and
  after hardware changes as incompatible.
- Participants are mostly white and highly educated, which limits generalisability; ADNI4 aims to change this.
- Sequences beyond T1, FLAIR and T2* are not available for all participants in ADNI2.
