The Infant Brain Imaging Study (IBIS) is a longitudinal MRI and behavioral study run by the IBIS Network, an NIH
Autism Center of Excellence. It follows infants who have an older sibling with autism, and so a raised familial
likelihood of autism themselves, together with low-risk infants, from 6 to 24 months of age. The aim is to find brain
changes that come before the behavioral signs of autism. Imaging and behavioral data are shared through NIMH Data
Archive collection 19, which lists 1039 subjects with shared data.

## Composition

Infants were enrolled at 6 months and returned at 12 and 24 months. Exclusion criteria included prematurity, low
birth weight, major medical or sensory conditions, contraindications for MRI, adoption and a first-degree relative
with intellectual disability, psychosis, schizophrenia or bipolar disorder. Autism outcome is assessed at 24 months
with the ADOS, and development at each visit with the Mullen Scales of Early Learning. Published analyses use subsets:
Hazlett et al. (2012) compared 98 high-risk and 36 low-risk infants at 6 months, and Wolff et al. (2012) followed 92
high-risk infants with diffusion data, 28 of whom met ASD criteria at 24 months. The NDA record does not break the
shared subjects down by group, age, sex or visit.

## Acquisition

Four clinical sites (UNC Chapel Hill, University of Washington, Children's Hospital of Philadelphia and Washington
University in St. Louis) used identical Siemens 3T TIM Trio scanners with 12-channel head coils, with data
coordinated at the Montreal Neurological Institute. Infants were scanned in natural sleep without sedation. The
protocol has 1 mm isotropic 3D T1w MPRAGE and 3D T2w FSE scans and a 25-direction DTI at 2 mm isotropic with b-values
up to 1000 s/mm². Monthly phantom scans and yearly traveling human phantoms tracked scanner stability across sites.

## Annotations

No manual image labels are described. A pediatric neuroradiologist reviews the scans for incidental findings, and
diffusion data pass automated quality control.

## Known limitations

- The high-risk group is defined by family history, so most infants are siblings of children with autism; low-risk
  controls are fewer in the published analyses.
- Later IBIS follow-ups (school age, adolescence) and newer IBIS studies are not described by NDA collection 19.
- Release dates and per-release contents are not published.
