The Aging Brain: Vasculature, Ischemia, and Behavior Study (ABVIB) is a program project funded by the National
Institute on Aging (P01 AG12435) and led by Helena C. Chui at the University of Southern California. It started in
1994 and asks how cardiovascular risk factors and cerebrovascular disease shape brain structure and cognition in older
adults, on their own and together with Alzheimer disease. Its database is held on the LONI Image and Data Archive and
is opened to researchers who submit a data use application.

## Composition

According to the data use agreement, the database holds data from a second study cohort enrolled from 2008 to 2013.
Measures include laboratory tests of cardiovascular risk, carotid intima media thickness, retinal vessel measures,
serial MRI and neuropsychological testing, with amyloid PET for part of the participants. The study does not publish
how many participants, visits or scans the shared database contains, so no counts are listed here.

## Acquisition

The program's PiB substudy (Marchant et al. 2013, 61 participants) describes the imaging used in that analysis: most
participants had T1-weighted MPRAGE and FLAIR on a Siemens 3 T Trio with an 8-channel head coil, a few were scanned on
a Siemens 4 T MedSpec or a GE 1.5 T Signa, and amyloid PET used [11C]PiB with 90 minutes of dynamic acquisition on a
high-resolution Siemens ECAT EXACT scanner. Whether the shared database follows the same protocol throughout is not
stated.

## Known limitations

- The program set out to recruit people with substantial vascular risk factors and vascular brain injury, so the
  cohort is not representative of the general older population.
- Scanners and field strengths differ between participants.
- Amyloid PET covers only a subset of participants.
- No public description of the released database exists beyond the data use agreement; contents must be checked
  after access is granted.
