This cohort comes from a University of Washington study led by Katie McLaughlin on how exposure to violence in
childhood shapes threat learning and later mental health. Children were scanned while they learned which coloured shape
predicted an unpleasant sound, and their symptoms were measured again about two years later. DeCross et al. (2022,
Biological Psychiatry) describe the cohort. Their OSF project was meant to hold the data, but it contains only the paper,
its supplement and figures, so the images are not available.

## Composition

159 children aged 8 to 16 (mean 12.6 years) were recruited in Seattle between January 2015 and June 2017 through
schools, community programmes, shelters, clinics and similar routes. The trauma group had experienced physical or
sexual abuse or had directly witnessed violence against a caregiver. Controls had no violence exposure and were matched
on age, sex and handedness. After excluding 12 children for an incomplete task, technical problems or motion, 147
remained: 77 trauma-exposed (40 girls) and 70 controls (33 girls). 121 of the 147 came back for the follow-up visit.
Symptoms of depression, anxiety, panic, PTSD and externalizing problems were rated with questionnaires and interviews.

## Acquisition

All scans used one 3T Philips Achieva with a 32-channel head coil at the UW Integrated Brain Imaging Center. Each child
had a T1-weighted MPRAGE at 1 mm isotropic resolution and gradient-echo EPI BOLD during the conditioning task (TR 2 s,
37 slices of 3 mm). The task presented four block types (CS-, reinforced CS+, unreinforced CS+ and a fixation baseline),
each four times, with a grating sound as the aversive stimulus. Younger or anxious children did a mock scan first.

## Annotations

No image annotations. Labels are trauma group, sex, age, race and ethnicity, family income and dimensional symptom
scores at baseline and follow-up.

## Known limitations

- Not shared. The paper points to the OSF project for data, but no imaging or tabular data are posted there, and no
  terms or request route are published.
- Sex and age breakdowns are given only for the 147 analysed children, not for all 159 scanned.
- The trauma and control groups differ strongly in race, ethnicity and family income.
- Only follow-up questionnaires are described; the paper does not report follow-up scans.
