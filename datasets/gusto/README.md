Growing Up in Singapore Towards healthy Outcomes (GUSTO) is a mother-offspring birth cohort run by the National
University of Singapore, KK Women's and Children's Hospital (KKH) and A\*STAR. Its first aim was to study how early-life
factors shape the risk of obesity and type 2 diabetes, and it has grown into a multi-domain study that also covers
allergy, neurodevelopment and mental health. Brain MRI is collected in a subset of the children, once shortly after
birth and repeatedly through childhood. Data and biospecimens are shared through collaboration with the cohort
investigators.

## Composition


Pregnant women of Chinese, Malay or Indian background, who were Singapore citizens or permanent residents, were
recruited at their first-trimester ultrasound visit at the National University Hospital and KKH between June 2009 and
September 2010. Mothers on chemotherapy or psychotropic drugs, or with type 1 diabetes, were excluded. Published papers
give different totals for the number of women recruited (1,247 in a 2024 paper, 1,450 in a 2025 paper), so no total is
listed here. A 2014 imaging paper reports that 189 GUSTO infants took part in the neonatal neuroimaging study. The child
scans are reported only for analysis samples, for example 296 children with 642 usable T1w scans and 256 children with
509 usable resting-state scans in a 2024 paper.

## Acquisition

Neonates were scanned at 5 to 17 days after birth on a 1.5 T GE scanner at KKH, in natural sleep without sedation, with
fast spin-echo T2w imaging and single-shot echo-planar DTI (19 directions at b = 600 s/mm², 3 mm slices). Children were
scanned at 4.5, 6, 7.5 and 10.5 years at 3 T: a Siemens Magnetom Skyra at 4.5 and 6 years and a Siemens Magnetom Prisma
at 7.5 and 10.5 years. The child protocol includes a 1 mm isotropic T1w MPRAGE and resting-state fMRI.

## Annotations

No manual labels are described. A neuroradiologist reviewed the neonatal brain scans.

## Known limitations

- Only a subset of the cohort was scanned, and no source that could be opened gives cohort-wide counts per imaging wave.
- The neonatal and child scans differ in field strength, vendor and contrasts; the neonatal wave has no T1w scan.
- The collaboration guidelines and data catalogue could not be read, so the terms of use are unknown.
