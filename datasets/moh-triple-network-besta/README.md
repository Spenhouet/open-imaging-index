A case-control cohort from the headache and neuroradiology units of the Carlo Besta neurological institute in Milan, enrolled in the MIND-CM project. Patients with chronic migraine who overuse acute headache drugs were scanned before a structured withdrawal treatment, together with healthy volunteers. The study asked how connectivity within and between three large brain networks (default mode, salience and central executive) differs in these patients, and how it relates to the type of overused drug, skin hypersensitivity during attacks (allodynia), anxiety and depression. The authors deposited the data behind the article as a restricted Zenodo record.

## Composition

126 participants were analysed: 91 patients with chronic migraine and medication overuse headache (80 women, mean age 46.5 years) and 35 healthy controls (30 women, mean age 43.5 years). Of 94 eligible patients, three were dropped for artifacts or head motion. By overuse profile there were 28 triptan users, 40 users of non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and 23 who overused several drug classes. Questionnaires covered allodynia, depression, anxiety, headache impact and drug intake over three months.

## Acquisition

All participants were scanned on one 3 T Philips Achieva dStream system with a 32-channel head coil, between attacks. The protocol included a 1 mm isotropic 3D T1-weighted image and an eyes-open resting-state echo-planar fMRI run of 450 volumes with 3 mm voxels.

## Annotations

No image labels. The Zenodo record describes a spreadsheet with age, sex, overused medications, mood and allodynia scores, chronification, disease duration, drug intake and within- and between-network connectivity values for each participant.

## Known limitations

The record is restricted, lists no files and states no license, so it is not clear whether the raw images can be obtained or only the derived connectivity table. The cohort comes from a single centre and is mostly female. The authors note that the findings still need to be checked in episodic migraine and in chronic migraine without drug overuse.
