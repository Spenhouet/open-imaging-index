ABIDE I was the first large open collection of brain MRI in autism. Seventeen sites in the US and Europe pooled
resting-state fMRI and structural scans they had already acquired, together with diagnostic and phenotypic
information, and released them through the 1000 Functional Connectomes Project / INDI in August 2012. It is widely
used to study functional connectivity in autism and as a benchmark for classifying autism from brain scans.

## Composition

The release holds 1,112 datasets: 539 from people with autism spectrum disorder and 573 from typical controls, aged
roughly 7 to 64 years with a median of 14.7. The 17 sites contributed 20 samples, ranging from 27 to 184 people each.
Every dataset has one resting-state fMRI run and one T1-weighted anatomical scan. Phenotypic data include diagnosis
(autistic disorder, Asperger's or PDD-NOS), ADOS and ADI-R scores where available, IQ, handedness, medication and,
for some sites, eye status during the rest scan.

## Acquisition

Each site used its own scanner and protocol; the site profiles name Siemens (Allegra, Trio, Verio), Philips and GE
systems. Resting-state instructions differed: some sites asked for eyes open with a fixation cross, others eyes
closed. Most sites screened controls for psychiatric conditions and matched them to the autism group on age and sex
at group level.

## Annotations

There are no image labels. Diagnoses come from each site's clinical assessment, mostly based on DSM-IV-TR with ADOS
and ADI-R.

## Known limitations

- Data were collected for different original studies, so inclusion criteria, age ranges and protocols vary strongly by
  site, and site effects are large.
- The sample is mostly male; a quarter of sites excluded females by design.
- Sex and detailed age distributions for the whole release are only available in the phenotypic file, which is
  distributed under the same non-commercial license after registration.
- Some participants also appear in other repositories: site notes mention overlap with ADHD-200 (controls) and
  NDAR (some children with autism).
