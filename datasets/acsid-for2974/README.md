ACSID is a research unit (FOR2974) funded by the German Research Foundation that studies the psychological and neural mechanisms behind gaming, pornography use, buying-shopping and social-networks use when these become addictive. Teams at Duisburg-Essen, Bochum, Bamberg, Giessen, Mainz, Lübeck and Hannover test participants with unproblematic, risky and pathological use with one shared core battery of tasks, questionnaires, biomarkers and a six-month follow-up. The website reports more than 1,200 participants in total, but most of that work is behavioural. Brain imaging is limited to a few subprojects that add task fMRI, so this entry covers only those imaging data, which are not public.

## Composition

The one fMRI sample with a published size comes from subproject RP3, which looks at appetitive conditioning and extinction. It enrolled 83 men: 25 with gaming disorder, 23 with risky gaming and 35 non-problematic users. A paper on the healthy control group of the same project analysed 31 men aged 19 to 38. The subproject pages describe further fMRI work: cue reactivity in gaming and pornography use disorders (RP4) in the first funding period, and punishment sensitivity (RP3), stress and habitual cue reactivity (RP4) and cue reactivity in buying-shopping and social-networks use disorders (RP10) in the second. Their completed sample sizes are not published.

## Acquisition

The RP3 controls were scanned on a 3 T Siemens MAGNETOM Prisma with a 64-channel head coil. Each session had a T1-weighted structural scan and whole-brain T2*-weighted echo-planar BOLD imaging (3 mm voxels, TR 2 s) during a conditioning task. Gaming, pornographic and monetary rewards served as unconditioned stimuli, and skin conductance and ratings were recorded alongside. RP10 reuses the cue-reactivity fMRI protocol of the first-period RP4 study.

## Annotations

None. The data are task fMRI with behavioural and questionnaire measures, not labelled images.

## Known limitations

No imaging data are public. The OSF project holds preregistrations and materials only. Data are available on request from the study authors. The published imaging samples are small and male-only, and no total imaging count for the research unit is published. The scanner is reported only for the RP3 control group.
