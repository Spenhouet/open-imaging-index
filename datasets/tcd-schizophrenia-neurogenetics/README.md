This entry covers the brain MRI sample of a neuroimaging genetics study of schizophrenia run at the Trinity College
Institute of Neuroscience in Dublin and funded by Science Foundation Ireland. The study's title was "A structural and
functional MRI investigation of genetics, cognition and emotion in schizophrenia". The images later became one of the
legacy datasets of the EU PSYSCAN project and were used in multi-cohort work on cortical morphometric similarity and on
machine-learning classification of schizophrenia.

## Composition

Patients came through local clinical services in Dublin, and controls with no psychiatric history were recruited by
local advertisement in the same area. Pregnancy, neurological disease, intellectual disability and recent substance
misuse were exclusion criteria. Morgan et al. (2019) started from 184 people with both T1-weighted and diffusion scans.
After removing 9 for head motion and 60 for poor diffusion data, 115 remained: 33 patients (30 schizophrenia, 3
schizoaffective disorder) and 82 controls. Patients were older on average (42.2 vs 33.5 years) and more often male
(73% vs 43%). Lei et al. (2020) used 32 patients and 83 controls with structural and resting-state functional scans.
How the two subsets overlap is not reported.

## Acquisition

All scans were made on one 3T Philips Intera Achieva system. The T1-weighted scan used TR/TE 8.4/3.8 ms and 0.9 mm
isotropic voxels. Resting-state fMRI had 180 volumes at TR 2 s with 3.5 mm isotropic voxels. Diffusion-weighted
imaging was also acquired, but its protocol is not described in the open sources. Symptoms were rated with SAPS and SANS
for 24 of the 33 analysed patients.

## Annotations

No image annotations. Labels are diagnosis, age, sex and symptom scores.

## Known limitations

- Images are not shared. Lei et al. (2020) state the data are unavailable for confidentiality reasons, and no terms are
  published.
- Only derived regional cortical measures (thickness, volume, area, curvature, FA, MD) with age, sex and group for the
  115 analysed participants are public, on Figshare under CC BY 4.0.
- The full study enrolment is not reported; the counts here cover the subsets used in two papers.
- Many controls were lost to diffusion quality control, so the analysed sample is unbalanced in age and sex.
