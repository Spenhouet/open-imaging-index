This OpenNeuro release holds the shared data of a 2025 PLoS One study by Cardinale, Brotman and colleagues at the
NIMH Intramural Research Program. The study tested CALM-IT, a mobile game in which children swipe targets and hold back
on stars, as a quick measure of inhibitory control. Its scores were compared with symptom questionnaires, four lab
tasks (anti-saccade, AX continuous performance, flanker and stop-signal) and brain activity during a flanker task in
the scanner. The data are shared under CC0.

## Composition

The paper analysed 200 youth aged 8 to 20 referred with an anxiety disorder, disruptive mood dysregulation disorder
(DMDD), ADHD, irritability below the DMDD threshold, or no diagnosis. Of these, 160 agreed to public sharing and are
in the release, with age, sex, race, ethnicity, parental education and income. Diagnostic group is not part of the
shared participant table, so the group sizes in the paper (for example 56 with ADHD) refer to all 200, not to the
release. MRI was optional: the paper reports 77 youth with a flanker fMRI scan. The release has 160 participants, of whom 39 were scanned and have
images. Parent-reported anxiety (SCARED), irritability (ARI) and ADHD (CBRS) items, CALM-IT measures and lab task
data, including anti-saccade eye tracking, are included for those who completed them.

## Acquisition

All images come from a 3T GE Discovery MR750 with a 32-channel head coil at the NIH. Each imaged participant has one
1 mm MPRAGE T1-weighted scan and four echo-planar BOLD runs of a modified Eriksen flanker task (TR 2 s, 3 mm axial
slices), with event files for each run.

## Annotations

There are no image labels. Task events, behavioural responses and questionnaire scores serve as the targets.

## Known limitations

- Only 39 of 160 shared participants have MRI, and the paper does not state the size of its analysed fMRI sample.
- Diagnostic group membership is not shared per participant.
- Single site and scanner; mostly White participants from one research hospital.
