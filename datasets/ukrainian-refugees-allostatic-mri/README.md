This entry covers a cross-sectional study run by the Department of Psychiatry at Wroclaw Medical University in Poland.
It compared adults who fled Ukraine after the Russian invasion of February 2022 with healthy controls of similar age
and sex, looking at trauma exposure, coping, psychiatric symptoms, the physiological wear of chronic stress (allostatic
load) and brain structure. The university's research data repository holds an anonymized participant-level table
under CC BY 4.0, which includes selected processed structural MRI variables. The scans themselves are not deposited;
the study papers say the raw data will be made available by the authors.

## Composition

The deposit describes 60 refugees and 50 controls. Refugees were 49 women and 11 men with a mean age of 27.8 years;
controls were 36 women and 14 men with a mean age of 26.1 years. In the cortical thickness analysis, 34 refugees had
PTSD symptoms and 22 did not, which sums to 56 rather than 60 without explanation in that paper. Participants were
recruited through social media and institutional websites.

## Acquisition

Brain MRI was acquired on a 3 T Philips Ingenia with a high-resolution sagittal T1-weighted sequence. Scan parameters
are not reported. Cortical thickness for 68 Desikan-Killiany regions was computed with CAT12 in SPM12. Interviews took
place on the day of scanning or shortly before. The tables also hold questionnaires on PTSD,
anxiety, depression, insomnia, affect, coping, resilience and lifetime trauma, plus blood pressure, heart rate, lipids,
glucose markers, cortisol, DHEAS and inflammatory markers.

## Annotations

There are no image labels. The tables give questionnaire scores, the allostatic load index, a coping flexibility index
and group status.

## Known limitations

- Only derived tables are public; the MRI scans must be requested from the authors and no terms are published.
- The papers report MRI results only for the refugees, so how many controls were scanned is not stated.
- Sex counts in the cortical thickness paper are inconsistent between its sections.
- Small single-site sample from one city.
