This OpenNeuro release (ds006905) holds the MRI of a clinical trial at NYU Langone Health that tested whether
Organizational Skills Training (OST), a behavioral program for children who struggle to track assignments, manage
materials and plan tasks, changes functional brain connectivity. Children were scanned at baseline and again after the
10-week intervention, or after a matched waiting period in the control arm. The trial ran in two phases, an open
single-arm phase and a randomized waitlist-controlled phase (NCT03148782 and NCT04108273). The data are shared under a
CC0 waiver.

## Composition

The release covers 84 children: 30 from the open phase and 54 from the randomized phase, each with complete data for at
least one viewing condition. The participants table lists 30 girls and 54 boys aged 7 to 11 years, with 57 children in
the treatment arm and 27 on the waitlist. All had deficient organizational skills on the parent-rated Children's
Organizational Skills Scale (COSS-P) in the context of neurodevelopmental disorders; 73 are coded as having ADHD. The
table also gives COSS parent and child scores before and after the intervention, IQ, two language scores and free-text
diagnoses.

## Acquisition

All scans were acquired on a Siemens 3T Prisma with a 32-channel head coil after mock-scanner practice. Each session
has a 0.8 mm isotropic MPRAGE with prospective motion correction from the ABCD protocol and up to three 7-minute
BOLD runs (TR 800 ms, 2.4 mm voxels): fixation on a cross, cartoon film clips, and the Inscapes abstract
animation. EPI fieldmaps are included for part of the runs.

## Known limitations

- Single site and scanner, and a modest sample.
- Not every child has all three viewing conditions in both sessions; there are 469 BOLD runs out of a possible 504.
- The authors note that fieldmaps are missing for several functional runs and refer users to the NIMH Data Archive copy
  for correct files.
- Ages are given in whole years. One child is listed as 7 although the stated inclusion range is 8 to 12 years.
- The ADHD column is coded Y or N with a presentation letter and is not described in a sidecar file.
- The linked paper analyses a subset of 46 children from the randomized phase, so its demographics differ from the
  release.
