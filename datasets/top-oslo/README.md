This cohort is the bipolar disorder part of the TOP study run by NORMENT at the University of Oslo and Oslo University
Hospital, labelled TOP3 as a Psychiatric Genomics Consortium substudy. Tesli et al. (2015) scanned people with bipolar
disorder and healthy controls with an emotional faces-matching fMRI task and related brain activation to diagnosis and
to a polygenic risk score for bipolar disorder computed from genome-wide genotypes. The raw images are not public; the
paper states they can be requested from the study, and 16 group z maps are on NeuroVault.

## Composition

The analysed sample has 206 adults aged 18 to 63: 85 with bipolar disorder (43 type I, 36 type II, 6 not otherwise
specified) and 121 healthy controls drawn at random from national population records in the same catchment area.
Women made up 61% of patients and 44% of controls. Mean age was about 35 years in both groups. Participants were
included between 2003 and 2009 and are of Northern European, mainly Norwegian, ancestry.

## Acquisition

All scans come from one 1.5 T Siemens Magnetom Sonata at Oslo University Hospital with a standard head coil. The task
fMRI used gradient-echo EPI (TR 2040 ms, TE 50 ms, 24 axial 4 mm slices, 3 mm in-plane) over two sessions, each
alternating blocks of angry or fearful faces, or happy faces, with a shape-matching control. A sagittal T1-weighted
MPRAGE (about 1.33 x 0.94 x 1 mm) was acquired twice and averaged for co-registration.

## Annotations

There are no image labels. Each participant has a SCID-based DSM-IV diagnosis, genotypes and a polygenic risk score,
IQ (WASI), task response times and accuracy, and for patients mood and symptom ratings (IDS, YMRS, PANSS positive),
functioning (GAF), illness history and medication on the scan day.

## Known limitations

- Raw images are released only on request under ethics restrictions, with no published terms.
- Mood and symptom ratings at scanning are missing for part of the patients (IDS for 60 of 85).
- Many patients were on antipsychotics, anticonvulsants, antidepressants or lithium.
- Counts describe only the sample analysed in this paper.
