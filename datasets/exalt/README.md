EXALT was a 26-week, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial run at four alcohol outpatient clinics in Copenhagen,
Denmark, testing whether the GLP-1 receptor agonist exenatide (2 mg once weekly) lowers drinking in treatment-seeking
adults with alcohol use disorder. Of 127 enrolled patients, a subgroup had brain fMRI and dopamine transporter SPECT
before randomization and again after treatment. The clinical tables are open on Mendeley Data under CC BY 4.0, but the
raw images are held back and offered only on request to the corresponding authors.

## Composition

The fMRI substudy reports 61 patients at baseline (33 placebo, 28 exenatide; 36 men; mean age 50.2) and 25 healthy
controls matched for sex, age and education (15 men; mean age 49.3). Patients were 18 to 70 years old and met DSM-5
alcohol use disorder and ICD-10 alcohol dependence criteria. The fMRI group analyses at week 26 include 33 patients
(16 placebo, 17 exenatide). For SPECT there were 46 baseline scans, one excluded for technical issues, plus 16
per-protocol and 5 premature rescans; every SPECT patient also took part in the fMRI substudy. A further 21 SPECT
controls came from earlier studies and are not part of this trial.

## Acquisition

MRI ran on a 3 T Siemens Prisma with a 64-channel head coil at Rigshospitalet: localizer, T1-weighted structural image
(0.9 mm slices), B0 field map, resting-state BOLD and two task runs, an alcohol cue-reactivity task (357 volumes) and
a spatial N-back working memory task (230 volumes), with TR 2 s and TE 30 ms. Controls were scanned once. SPECT used
about 185 MBq of [123I]FP-CIT and a triple-head Philips IRIX camera, acquiring six 10-minute frames between 180 and
240 minutes after injection, with cerebellum as reference region for striatal binding potential.

## Known limitations

- The images have no public download, license or published access terms; access depends on approval by the authors.
- Many patients left the trial early (69 of 127), so few have both baseline and week-26 scans.
- Single scanner and single SPECT camera at one hospital.
- Patients were all White and recruited in one city.
- The paper and the CONSORT figure legend disagree on which arm received 62 and which 65 patients.
