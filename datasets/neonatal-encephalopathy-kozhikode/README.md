This entry describes the brain MRI cohort of a prospective study at Government Medical College, Kozhikode, in Kerala,
India, run with Imperial College London and University College London. Term and near-term newborns with perinatal
asphyxia and early signs of encephalopathy were recruited over six months in 2009 and imaged in their first weeks of
life. The aim was to learn whether brain injury in this low- and middle-income setting starts around birth, and so
could be treatable by cooling, and how imaging relates to development at about three and a half years. The images
have not been released, and the paper has no data availability statement.

## Composition

The study recruited 54 infants with a Thompson encephalopathy score above 5 within six hours of birth. Six died before
discharge. Of the 48 survivors, 47 had MRI and one had CT only. Images of three infants were lost before analysis, so
44 infants had scans that were reported. Thirty-one of them had diffusion tensor data of good quality and 22 had
thalamic spectroscopy, 20 with both. Seventeen infants in the later part of the study were randomised to whole-body
cooling. Thirty-eight survivors were assessed at a mean age of 3.4 years with the Bayley III scales and a neurological
examination.

## Acquisition

Scans were done on a 1.5 T Siemens Avanto between one and three weeks after birth, at a mean of 9.3 days. The protocol
had 3D T1-weighted gradient echo, T2-weighted fast spin echo in two planes, 20-direction diffusion tensor imaging with
5 mm axial slices, and single-voxel proton spectroscopy in the left thalamus.

## Annotations

A perinatal neurologist, blinded to clinical data and outcome, scored injury in white matter, cortex, basal ganglia
and thalami and the posterior limb of the internal capsule. White matter injury was seen in 40 infants, cortical
injury in 31 and deep grey matter injury in 12. Fractional anisotropy was compared between groups with tract-based
spatial statistics, and metabolite peak-area ratios were fitted from the spectra.

## Known limitations

- No images, scores or derived maps are shared, and no terms for them are published.
- The six infants who died, probably the most severely injured, were not imaged.
- There is no control group of healthy newborns.
- DTI slice thickness varied between 5 and 6.5 mm across infants.
