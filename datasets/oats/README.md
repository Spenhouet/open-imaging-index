The Older Australian Twins Study (OATS) is a longitudinal study of older twins run by the Centre for Healthy Brain
Ageing (CHeBA) at UNSW Sydney, with sites in New South Wales, Queensland and Victoria. Comparing identical and non-identical twins lets
it separate genetic from environmental influences on brain ageing, cognitive decline and dementia. Participants were
recruited through the Australian Twin Registry and seen up to six times until assessments stopped in 2021. Researchers
apply for the data through Dementias Platform Australia (DPAU).

## Composition

The baseline cohort has 623 people aged 65 or older: 161 identical and 124 non-identical twin pairs, one identical
triplet set, 27 single twins and 23 siblings who are not twins. CHeBA reports 450 at the second assessment (2009 to
2013) and 392 at the third (2012 to 2016); a 2013 paper gives 426 for wave 2. By 2017 the study had obtained 1,126
MRI brain scans. An amyloid PET sub-study scanned 207 participants in New South Wales and Victoria between 2012 and
2017. Assessments also cover cognition, psychiatric and cardiovascular health, blood biochemistry and genetics.

## Acquisition

The SWI used for microbleed work was acquired on a 1.5 T Siemens MAGNETOM Avanto with a 3D gradient-echo sequence
(TR 38 ms, TE 30 ms, flip angle 15 degrees, 1.5 mm slices). Scanners and protocols for the other MRI sequences and
sites are not summarised in the sources used here.

## Annotations

Hassanzadeh et al. (2025) had two radiologists independently mark cerebral microbleeds on an OATS SWI subset and
report 54 microbleeds (23 scans in the dataset section, 24 in the results). DPAU lists MRI microbleeds as an OATS data
category; whether it holds these labels is not stated.

## Known limitations

- Wave sizes differ between the CHeBA website and the 2013 paper.
- The number of participants with each MRI sequence, and the sex and age distribution, are not published in the
  sources used here.
- Access costs an application fee, is limited to an approved project, and raw MRI is not suited to DPAU's analysis
  environment.
