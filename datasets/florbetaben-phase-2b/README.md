This entry covers the participants of a phase IIb diagnostic trial of florbetaben (BAY 94-9172), an [18F] tracer that
binds amyloid plaque, as reported by Schipke and colleagues in 2012. Their add-on study asked the physicians who had
referred each participant how confident they were in the clinical diagnosis before the scan, how the florbetaben
result compared with it and whether it would change patient management. The PET images themselves have not been
released; only the paper and a figshare record with an erratum and supplementary tables are public.

## Composition

The full phase IIb analysis set held 272 people: 147 with probable Alzheimer's disease by NINCDS-ADRDA criteria
(74 men, 73 women, mean age 73.9 years, mean MMSE 23.0) and 125 cognitively unimpaired controls (73 men, 52 women,
mean age 70.7 years, mean MMSE 29.4). All controls had a CDR of 0; the patients had CDR 0.5 (60), 1 (77) or 2 (10).
Referring physicians returned questionnaires for 121 patients and 80 controls. Centres in Australia, Germany, Japan
and Switzerland took part in the questionnaire study.

## Acquisition

Every participant underwent brain PET with florbetaben. Scanner models, acquisition timing and reconstruction
settings are not stated in the public parts of the sources.

## Annotations

Scans were read visually and graded with the brain amyloid plaque load (BAPL) score as normal, minor plaque or
significant plaque. Among the questionnaire cases, 18% of the scans of patients with probable Alzheimer's disease were read as negative and 18% of the control scans as positive, and the abstract reports increased diagnostic confidence in 80%.

## Known limitations

- No images, reads or participant-level data are available, and no access route is published.
- The full text of the paper is behind a paywall; the numbers here come from its abstract and its openly licensed
  supplement.
- The trial registration number is not given in the public sources, so this entry is not linked to a registry
  record.
- The supplement lists the questionnaire countries but not the number of centres or questionnaires per country.
