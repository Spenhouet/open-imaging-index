This entry covers the imaging sample of a clinical study of error processing at Massachusetts General Hospital and the
Martinos Center in Boston. Agam et al. (PLoS ONE 2014) had the same people perform an antisaccade task once during
functional MRI and once during simultaneous MEG and EEG, then asked whether two genetic variants, DRD4 C-521T and
MTHFR C677T, act differently on two error signals: error-related activation of the dorsal anterior cingulate in fMRI
and the error-related negativity in EEG. The images were not released; they can be requested from the corresponding
author.

## Composition

Of 144 enrolled participants, 105 completed both the fMRI and the MEG/EEG session, and 92 had at least 10 usable error
trials in each. These 92 (62 male, mean age 36, SD 13) comprise 33 healthy adults, 28 people with schizophrenia, 18 with
obsessive-compulsive disorder and 13 with autism spectrum disorder. Each was genotyped from saliva for the two variants,
and 74 were classed as Caucasian by self-report and ancestry markers. Fifty of them were part of an earlier analysis of
MTHFR and error-related activation.

## Acquisition

MRI used a Siemens Trio at 3 T, with a 12-channel head coil for 82 participants and a 32-channel coil for 10 healthy
participants. The protocol had a sagittal MP-RAGE, a multi-echo FLASH used to build the head model for MEG/EEG source
estimation, and six gradient-echo BOLD runs of the antisaccade task (TR 2 s), with eye tracking in the scanner. The
same task was run eight times during MEG (306-sensor Neuromag VectorView) and 70-channel EEG.

## Annotations

No image annotations. Per-participant labels in the study are diagnosis, genotype, antisaccade error rate and reaction
time, error-related dACC activation and ERN amplitude.

## Known limitations

- Not public. Access is on request for researchers who meet the criteria of the Partners Human Research Committee; no
  terms are published.
- The figshare record linked to the paper holds only a supplementary source-estimate figure and a table, no scans.
- Sex is reported only for the whole sample, and age only as mean and SD.
- MEG and EEG recordings are part of the study but have no modality term in this index.
